Benchmarks

A professional drummer, recorded against a metronome, landed individual hits 10 to 20 ms ahead of or behind the click (Hennig et al. 2011, written up in the Harvard Gazette). Listeners start to notice drum timing shifts at around 20 ms.

So under 20 ms of spread is pro territory, and 15 ms is tight by anyone's standard.

How it's measured

Hats (all openness variants), hat foot and kick are measured to the nearest 8th note; snare to the nearest 16th, with beats 2 and 4 counted as backbeats and everything else as ghost notes. Soft double triggers (a repeat within 80 ms at under 60% of the previous velocity) are dropped first. Spread is the standard deviation; robust spread (1.4826 × median absolute deviation) is in the table. Ride, crash and toms are not tracked yet.

Numbers include a small fixed e-kit latency, so absolute lean reads slightly late. Comparing limbs and weeks is still fair.
